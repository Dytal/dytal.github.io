THE STORY BEHIND THE FLIGHT

About Sky Hopper

Sky Hopper is an original browser arcade game made to turn a simple one-button idea into a small, replayable flight challenge. It runs in a modern browser without a separate download or installer.

Who made it?

Sky Hopper is developed and maintained by Anish Sandeep Bhargav, known online as Dytalmc. The project brings together the gameplay code, interface, Canvas-rendered world, relic effects, progression system and player-facing documentation in one static web application.

The game’s bird, gates, skies, particles and effects are rendered with original Canvas drawing routines and CSS rather than embedding someone else’s game. The experience is designed around short attempts: learn the movement, understand the upcoming obstacle, and try again with a better rhythm.

What is different about the two modes?

Classic strips the challenge back to timing. The bird has no health bar and no manual power abilities; one collision ends the run. Gates slowly become quicker as the score grows. Classic does not grant coins, so it remains a direct test of control rather than a currency strategy.

Arcade adds a three-heart starting health pool, collectible relics and three abilities. Difficulty rises through Normal, Swift, Hard, Expert and Chaos. The relics are organised into protection, time, healing, attraction, movement and score effects. Their drop chances and effects are described inside the relic codex so the collection is more than a row of unexplained icons.

How the progression system works

Signed-in players can keep their profile, best score, flight totals, XP, achievements, relic inventory, upgrades, cosmetics and equipped appearance in the configured Supabase database. Guest play is open without an account, but guest progression lives in page memory and is reset when the page is refreshed or closed. A guest’s chosen display name is saved separately on that device; it is not published to the global leaderboard.

Arcade points contribute to a cumulative coin counter. Every time the lifetime Arcade total crosses another 100-point threshold, one whole coin is credited. Classic runs, relic pickups, XP and best-score records do not award additional coins. Store prices are in-game prices only; the site does not implement real-money purchases.

Design principles

  • Play first: the core flight loop is available without signing in.
  • Explain the systems: mode descriptions, controls, relic effects and the coin rule are documented on the site.
  • Keep progression understandable: the store shows coin costs before a purchase and uses database functions for cloud coin deductions.
  • Respect player privacy: public pilot names are nicknames; the leaderboard does not intentionally display email addresses.
  • Improve through feedback: bug reports should include repeatable steps and the browser/device, but never passwords or personal information.

Current limitations

Sky Hopper is still a browser game, not a server-authoritative competitive platform. A determined player could manipulate client-side gameplay, so the global score system should not be treated as a secure esports ranking. Online account and progression features also require the project owner’s Supabase database and configuration to be available.
