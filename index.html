THE PILOT'S FIELD GUIDE

How to play Sky Hopper

Sky Hopper is an original one-button flying game. Your job is to guide the bird through each open gate, manage your rhythm, and learn how the two flight modes behave.

01 · CONTROL

Use short, deliberate flaps

Each flap gives the bird a quick upward impulse, then gravity pulls it down again. Repeatedly holding a key does not make the bird rise continuously; time each press and watch the bird's position rather than the score counter.

Keyboard: Space, ↑ or W. Mouse: click the play area. Touch: tap the game.

02 · TIMING

Plan for the next gate

Look through the gap before the gate reaches the bird. Aim for the middle of the opening instead of grazing either edge. If the next opening sits higher, start rising earlier; if it sits lower, stop flapping and let gravity do some of the work.

Pause: press P or Escape when a run is active.

03 · PRACTICE

Build consistency before speed

Classic is the best place to practise the basic rhythm without collectible effects. Arcade adds health, abilities, relics and more variable gate layouts. A steady sequence of safe crossings is more useful than chasing one risky pickup.

Restart: use the Restart button whenever you want a fresh attempt.

TWO FLIGHT STYLES

Choose the sky that suits you

Both modes use the same core movement, but they reward different decisions.

☼NO POWER-UPS

Classic mode

Classic is a clean test of timing and control. One collision ends the attempt, gates gradually become quicker, and there are no manual skills or relic pickups to distract you.

  • One hit ends the run.
  • Gate crossings add score.
  • Classic runs do not earn coins.

Good for: learning the flight arc and setting a personal best.

⚡RELICS & ABILITIES

Arcade mode

Arcade starts at Normal and moves through Swift, Hard, Expert and Chaos as your score rises. It gives you multiple hearts, collectible relics and three timed abilities, so planning matters as much as reflexes.

  • Use Q for Star Shield, E for Slow Skies and R for Heart Bloom.
  • Relics can grant protection, healing, lift, magnet reach, time effects or bonus points.
  • Earn one whole coin for every 100 cumulative Arcade points.

Good for: experimenting with abilities and collecting the relic codex.

ARCADE FIELD NOTES

Understand relics before you chase them

Relics appear as glowing symbols during Arcade flights. Each pickup has a fixed identity once it spawns; the colour and symbol help you recognise its effect.

Protection

Aegis and Ward relics create a protective shield or a brief window of invulnerability. They are useful when the upcoming gate looks awkward, but they are not a reason to deliberately hit a pipe.

Time and recovery

Hourglass and Time Shard relics slow the world for a short period. Heart relics restore a heart when you have room, or provide a short protective effect when your hearts are already full.

Movement and score

Magnet relics widen the pickup reach temporarily, Gust gives the bird an upward burst, and Star stores bonus points for later gate crossings. Score relics do not award coins directly.

Pickup safety: a relic is optional. Keep your bird clear of the gate first; do not change a safe flight path just to collect a glowing item. The full codex lists each relic's rarity, name and effect.

PROGRESSION WITHOUT CASH

How scores, coins and upgrades work

The game has no real-money checkout. Coins are a gameplay reward, and the store uses them for cosmetic styles and permanent upgrades.

The Arcade coin rule

Coins accumulate against your lifetime Arcade score, not separately for every short run. Crossing each 100-point threshold credits one coin; partial progress carries over. For example, 72 points in one flight and 35 in a later flight crosses the 100-point mark and earns one coin on the second flight.

Classic contributes zero to the Arcade total, so it never adds coins. Collecting a relic, earning XP or setting a best score does not create a separate coin bonus.

What the workshop changes

Cosmetics change the bird, aura, trail and wing appearance. Upgrades tune shield duration, cooldowns, starting hearts, relic attraction, rarity weighting, score bonuses or Gust lift. Prices are shown before purchase and are capped at 100 coins.

Signed-in purchases use database functions to check the balance before deducting coins. Guest progression is temporary and cannot make cloud purchases; create an account if you want long-term inventory and flight history.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Quick answers for pilots

Why did my guest score disappear?

Guest scores, coins, relic counts and achievements are stored only in the current page session. Your custom pilot name is saved separately on this device, but it does not save progression. Create and sign in to a pilot account to sync progression to the configured cloud database.

Can I change my pilot name later?

Yes. Use the Choose/Change pilot name control in the Pilot profile panel. Guest names are stored on the current device; signed-in names update the public profile and may be visible on the leaderboard. Use a nickname that does not disclose personal details.

Why can't I use abilities in Classic?

Classic is deliberately kept as a pure timing challenge. Select Arcade to unlock hearts, Q/E/R abilities and relic pickups.

Why did I receive no coin after a flight?

Only Arcade points contribute to coin rewards. A coin is credited when your cumulative Arcade score crosses another 100-point threshold, so a short run may add progress without awarding a whole coin immediately.

Do I need an account to play?

No. The core flight game is playable as a guest. An account is optional and is needed for permanent cloud saves, account inventory, cloud achievements and the shared profile leaderboard.

What should I do if the game or an item looks wrong?

Refresh the page first and try a new Arcade flight. If the problem repeats, note your browser, device, game mode and the steps that caused it, then use the Contact page to submit a bug report. Never include your password or other private information in a public report.
