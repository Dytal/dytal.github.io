PLAYER HANDBOOK

The Sky Hopper gameplay guide

This guide explains the controls, flight rhythm, difficulty system, Arcade abilities, relic types and the in-game currency rules. Use it as a practical reference while learning the game.

2. Understand the bird's movement

A flap changes the bird’s vertical velocity. Gravity continues between presses, so holding a key does not act like a jetpack. The bird follows an arc: a flap sends it upward, the upward movement slows, and then the bird begins to fall. Your task is to choose the next flap before the bird reaches an unsafe height.

Make small corrections

Large bursts of repeated input may carry the bird too high, while waiting until it has nearly reached the floor may leave too little time to recover. Try short intervals between flaps and notice how quickly the bird falls. The right rhythm depends on the gate position and current speed, so avoid memorising a single fixed pattern.

Look ahead, not at the score

The score changes after a gate has been passed. Watching the score is useful for tracking progress, but reacting to it can distract from the next opening. Keep your attention near the next gate and aim for the middle of the gap, where there is the most room above and below the bird.

3. Classic and Arcade modes

Classic: control without distractions

Classic has no health pool or active abilities. One collision ends the attempt, and the gates gradually get faster. It never awards coins. Use it to practise the movement, learn how the bird responds to each press and build a consistent score without worrying about collectibles.

Arcade: survive and adapt

Arcade starts at Normal and progresses through Swift, Hard, Expert and Chaos. Gate pacing, spacing, gap size and gap placement vary more as your score increases. You start with multiple hearts and can collect relics. When the difficulty rises, prioritise a safe gate crossing over a risky collectible.

4. The three abilities

  • Star Shield (Q): briefly protects against a hit. Use it when the next sequence looks difficult, not only after a collision has already occurred.
  • Slow Skies (E): reduces gate movement for a limited window. It can help you judge a changing gap and gives a little more time to correct your position.
  • Heart Bloom (R): restores one heart when you are below your current maximum. Using it at full health is unnecessary, so check the heart display first.

Abilities have cooldowns. The ability panel shows whether each skill is ready or cooling down. Upgrades can adjust some of these durations and cooldowns, and the game shows the current ready state in the interface.

5. Relic categories and rarity

Arcade relics are collectible effects with a visible symbol, glow colour and rarity. The catalogue contains six rarity tiers: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary and Mythic. The overall base probabilities shown in the codex are 48%, 28%, 15%, 6.5%, 2% and 0.5% respectively. These values describe the rarity roll; the particular relic within a tier is selected separately. The Rarity Compass upgrade changes higher-tier weighting slightly.

  • Aegis / Ward: protection and short invulnerability.
  • Hourglass / Time Shard: temporarily slow the world.
  • Heart: restore a heart or grant a brief ward at full health.
  • Magnet: temporarily increase collection reach.
  • Gust: push the bird upward.
  • Star: add bonus points to upcoming gate crossings.

Collecting any relic applies its effect and records the pickup for the active profile. A relic’s type is fixed when it spawns; the visual pulse and bobbing animation do not change its identity.

6. How the coin economy works

Only Arcade points contribute to the cumulative Arcade total. One whole coin is credited for every completed 100-point milestone, across multiple flights. The reward for a run is the difference between the number of 100-point milestones before and after that run.

Example: a player with 72 lifetime Arcade points scores 35 in a new Arcade flight. The total becomes 107, so one coin is earned. The extra seven points carry forward toward the next coin. A Classic run gives no coin, and neither XP nor item collection creates a separate coin reward.

The Bazaar sells bird looks, auras, trails, wing styles and permanent upgrades. These are bought with game-earned coins; there is no real-money checkout. The maximum listed cosmetic price is 100 coins, and prices are displayed before a purchase.

7. Guest play, pilot names and cloud saves

Guests can play without registering. Guest score, XP, coins, awards, item counts and run history are temporary and disappear after refresh or closing the page. A chosen guest display name is kept on the same browser/device as a preference, but it does not save progress or appear on the global leaderboard.

When Supabase is configured and you are signed in, the profile and progression systems can sync to the cloud. Your pilot name can be changed from the Pilot profile panel. The public leaderboard may show the name, best score and level, so choose a nickname that does not reveal your real-world identity.

8. Troubleshooting

  • Black or empty play area: reload the page, check that JavaScript is enabled and try a current version of Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
  • Cloud profile does not appear: check the public Supabase URL/key configuration, database schema and browser network connection. Never put a service-role key in the site.
  • Sign-in returns you to guest mode after refresh: the current setup keeps the auth session in page memory; signing in again can be expected.
  • Relic overlaps a pipe or appears incorrectly: refresh, start a new Arcade run and record the exact steps if it repeats. The latest build positions relics beyond the gate clearance zone.
  • Coins look unchanged: check your total Arcade points, not only the points in the current run. A coin appears only when the cumulative total crosses another 100-point threshold.
